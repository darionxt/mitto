- A Page, whose API stays private, navigates the frame that a link's or `window.open()`'s target names, `_top` and `_parent` those, as Chrome 157 does, where each navigated its own frame. A document navigates only the frames that Chrome lets it, with Chrome's errors: a navigation of the top-level frame that a frame's document starts is that document's, one to a fragment that another origin's document starts commits from a task and fires `load` at the frame's element, and a link in a frameset's frame to the frameset's fragment loads it again. A `file:` or `filesystem:` URL, a `blob:` URL of another origin and a URL of Chrome's own schemes go nowhere, with Chrome's error. A navigation's `Referer` is what its document's URL and policy gave as it started, where its target's `navigate` and `beforeunload` listeners could change it. Setting `opener` to `null` shadows it with a data property, where it kept the accessor, a named frame that `window.open()` opens has its `opener`, and `rel=noreferrer` and `noreferrer` send no `Referer`. A `srcdoc` that a document of another origin than its parent's asks for gives Chrome's error page, and a sandbox withholds `screen.orientation.lock()` and `PresentationRequest`, with Chrome's `SecurityError` ([runtime.md](docs/runtime.md#frames)).
